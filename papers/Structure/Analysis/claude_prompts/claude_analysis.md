### 2026-07-14 (Prompt 7 — why the scalar structure function is negative)

JXP asked whether the systematically negative scalar mixed moment is simply a consequence of \duL\
being negative. **It is not**, and I added a subsection (§8.1) to `new_analysis.tex` making the
case both structurally and empirically.

Structural argument: since $(\delta\theta)^2\ge0$, the reported quantity is the *centered* moment
$\langle u'q'^2\rangle$ with $u'=\duL-\langle\duL\rangle$, so $\langle u'\rangle=0$ and the mean
increment cannot set its sign. The exact decomposition is
$\langle\duL\,\delta\theta^2\rangle = S_1\langle\delta\theta^2\rangle + 2\bar q\langle u'q'\rangle
+ \langle u'q'^2\rangle$; only the reported third term survives centering, and it is negative only
if large scalar contrasts sit preferentially at converging ($u'<0$) pairs — genuine downgradient
transfer.

Empirical proof (new function `scalar_sf.decompose_mixed` + figure
`figs_scalar.fig_decomposition` → `fig_scalar_decomp_z60.png`): in **both Calypso surveys $S_1$ is
positive** (86–88% of bins; median +0.010 to +0.017 m/s), so the mean-flow term is positive, yet
